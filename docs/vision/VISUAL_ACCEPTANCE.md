# Visual Acceptance Standard v1

Grok's standard as supplied/adopted by Kyle and Atlas on 13 November 2026, with
all supplied practical refinements. No separate original standard document was
found on the preserved Grok branch; its technical HANDOVER.md remains unchanged.

## Overworld

The colony should read as a small illuminated settlement within an expansive
environment. Buildings must be recognisable without relying exclusively on labels.
People must perform visible genuine work. Day and night must feel meaningfully
different. The idle interface should generally cover no more than approximately
20% of the viewport. Reject obvious repetitive terrain, blurry sprites, excessive
UI obstruction and major visible frame stutters. Verify through paired screenshots
and short interaction recordings.

## Interiors

The original cutaway composition must remain recognisable. Important furniture
must not disappear through arbitrary cropping. No stretched art or repetitive
filler. Lighting must have convincing falloff. The window must agree with actual
time and weather. Entry and exit must work reliably. Home types must eventually
become visually distinct. Verify using side-by-side reference comparisons and
actual transition recordings.

## Characters

Residents must use coherent authored pixel artwork. Their poses must correspond
to real activities. Furniture and characters must layer correctly. Characters
should be recognisable across different views. Reject geometric placeholder
people, floating bodies, clipping, contradictory poses and visually incompatible
sprites. Verify with screenshots, magnified crops and real gameplay recordings.

## UI

Controls must belong to the same visual identity as the game world. Information
should be contextual and readable. Touch targets must be comfortably accessible.
Avoid the old black-and-gold, heavily bordered dashboard presentation. No interface
element should unnecessarily cover its own subject. Verify at multiple mobile
sizes and in several interaction states.

## Five final acceptance areas

1. **Reference fidelity:** gameplay reproduces the approved reference's composition,
   perspective and emotional atmosphere rather than merely copying its objects.
2. **Continuous interaction:** a real recording demonstrates exterior play,
   entering a home, observing genuine activity, inspecting a resident and exiting
   without visual or interaction failures.
3. **No player-facing placeholders:** approved gameplay scenes contain no crude
   geometric stand-ins, inappropriate scaling or artificial filler.
4. **Truthful day and night:** environmental lighting and windows agree with the
   game clock, weather and simulation.
5. **Real-save compatibility:** the experience works in an established colony
   without corrupting saves, breaking progression or inventing simulated behaviour.

## Practical refinements

Do not interpret the standard in ways that make ordinary development impossible.
Developer-only mockups and placeholders can be useful before visual acceptance.
A 60-second demonstration may use controlled game-time advancement where necessary,
but must not fake occupancy or events. Concept and gameplay need not be
pixel-identical: composition, perspective and emotional intent must agree.
The standard is a quality framework, not an excuse to avoid engineering work.

## Checkpoint A boundary

Approve/correct the complete home framing, outside context, truthful day/night,
controls and entry/exit first. Resident artwork is explicitly absent, not accepted.
Do not mark the five final acceptance areas satisfied by an empty-room composition
checkpoint. Do not start a broad animation rollout before Kyle/Atlas's review.
